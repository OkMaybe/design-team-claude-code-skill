# UX Psychology Principles

## 1. Cognitive Load and Decision Making

**Hick's Law**: Decision time increases logarithmically with choices.
Application: Reduce options per screen. Use progressive disclosure for complex flows.

**Miller's Law**: Working memory holds 7 plus or minus 2 items.
Application: Group navigation into max 7 categories. Chunk form fields logically.

**Cognitive Load Theory**: Intrinsic (task complexity), extraneous (poor design), germane (learning).
Application: Minimize extraneous load through clear layout and consistent patterns.

**Paradox of Choice**: Too many options leads to anxiety and decision paralysis.
Application: Curate options. Highlight recommended choice. Limit plan tiers to 3-4.

**Decision Fatigue**: Quality of decisions degrades after many choices.
Application: Put critical decisions early. Use smart defaults for non-critical settings.

## 2. Attention and Perception

**Pre-attentive Processing**: Color, size, orientation, motion detected in under 200ms.
Application: Use color and size for visual hierarchy. CTAs must be visually distinct.

**F-Pattern Scanning**: Users scan text-heavy pages in F-shape.
Application: Place key content along F path. Headlines and CTAs on left side.

**Z-Pattern Scanning**: Users scan simple pages in Z-shape.
Application: Logo top-left, CTA top-right, content diagonal, CTA bottom-right.

**Von Restorff Effect**: Distinctive items are more memorable.
Application: Make CTAs visually unique. Use contrast color for primary action.

**Banner Blindness**: Users ignore anything that looks like an ad.
Application: Avoid ad-like styling for important content.

**Serial Position Effect**: First and last items in a list are remembered best.
Application: Put important nav items first and last.

## 3. Motivation and Behavior

**Fitts's Law**: Time to reach target = distance / size.
Application: Make primary CTAs large, place in high-attention zones.

**Goal Gradient Effect**: People accelerate effort as they approach a goal.
Application: Show progress bars. Start at 10% not 0% to trigger momentum.

**Loss Aversion**: Losses feel ~2x stronger than equivalent gains.
Application: Frame around what users lose by not acting.

**Endowment Effect**: People value things more once they feel ownership.
Application: Free trials and personalization create sense of ownership.

**Peak-End Rule**: Experiences judged by peak moment and ending.
Application: Create a delight moment and end with positive confirmation.

**Zeigarnik Effect**: Incomplete tasks occupy the mind more.
Application: Progress bars for incomplete profiles keep users returning.

## 4. Trust and Persuasion

**Social Proof**: People follow the behavior of similar others.
Application: Testimonials, user counts, real-time notifications near CTAs.

**Authority Principle**: Expert endorsement increases credibility.
Application: Certifications, awards, expert quotes, media mentions.

**Scarcity Principle**: Limited availability increases perceived value.
Application: Limited seats, countdown timers. Must be authentic.

**Reciprocity**: Giving something free creates obligation.
Application: Free tools, valuable content before asking for signup.

**Anchoring Effect**: First number seen influences subsequent judgment.
Application: Show original price before discount. High-tier plan first.

**Framing Effect**: Same information presented differently changes perception.
Application: 95% satisfaction rate vs 5% complaint rate.

## 5. Gestalt Principles

**Proximity**: Elements close together perceived as related.
Application: Group related form fields. Whitespace between sections.

**Similarity**: Elements that look alike perceived as related.
Application: Consistent button styles for similar actions.

**Continuity**: Eyes follow smooth lines and curves.
Application: Align elements along clear visual lines.

**Closure**: Mind fills in gaps to see complete shapes.
Application: Partial borders, progress indicators leverage closure.

**Figure-Ground**: Objects perceived as foreground or background.
Application: Modal overlays, card elevation/shadow.

**Common Region**: Elements within a boundary are grouped.
Application: Cards, containers, backgrounds group related content.

## 6. Emotional Design

**Aesthetic-Usability Effect**: Beautiful things perceived as more usable.
Application: Visual polish directly impacts perceived reliability.

**Progressive Disclosure**: Show only what is needed now.
Application: Expandable sections, advanced settings behind toggles.

**Recognition over Recall**: Seeing options easier than remembering.
Application: Dropdowns over free text. Recent items. Autocomplete.
